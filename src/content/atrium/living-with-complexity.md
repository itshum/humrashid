A quieter, more nuanced companion to The Design of Everyday Things.
Norman's argument here is that complexity isn't the enemy - confusion
is. A washing machine with twenty settings isn't automatically worse
than one with three; what matters is whether the complexity maps to
something the task actually requires.

Worth reading specifically as a corrective to the "just make it
simpler" reflex, which doesn't always hold up once the underlying task
is genuinely complicated.
