Cagan's central complaint is that most companies run "feature teams" -
groups handed a roadmap of things to build - instead of real product
teams empowered to solve a problem however works best. The book is
basically a long argument for the second model, backed by decades of
consulting across companies that do it well and companies that don't.

It reads a little like a manifesto in places, but the distinction
between output (features shipped) and outcome (problems actually
solved) is one of those ideas that's obvious once stated and hard to
un-see afterward.
