Borrowing Brian Eno's term, Kelly argues that what looks like lone
genius in hindsight was usually a dense, supportive scene - a group of
peers trading ideas, competing, and building on each other's work in
the same place at the same time.

A useful corrective to the myth of the solitary genius, and a good
argument for why proximity to the right people still matters even when
everything can happen remotely.
