Horton and Quesenbery build the book around seven personas covering
different disabilities and situational limits, then walk through how
real design decisions land differently for each of them. It reframes
accessibility as a design problem with real users, not a legal
checklist run at the end of a project.

The most useful shift the book makes: accessible design keeps turning
out to be better design for everyone (captions help in a loud room,
clear contrast helps in bad lighting), not a tradeoff against it.
